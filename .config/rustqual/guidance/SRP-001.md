The struct scores as doing more than one job.
The score combines four things: how many groups its methods fall into when
methods that share a field or call each other are grouped (LCOM4), its field
count against 12, its method count against 20, and how many different functions
its methods call against 10.
The finding gives LCOM4, fields and methods, and names neither the groups nor
the score.

Fixes, in order:

1. With an LCOM4 of 3 or more, split the struct along its groups: each group of
   methods, with the fields it uses, becomes a type of its own.
2. A method that reads no field is a group by itself.
   Make it a free function, or an associated function without `self`.
3. With an LCOM4 of 1 and many fields or methods, move the fields that change
   together into a struct of their own, and their methods with them.

A struct whose shape something else dictates, such as a config section or a
clap command, can't be split to suit the score.
Say that the finding is of this kind and leave it.

Don't read a field in a method only to join two groups, and don't merge methods
to lower the count.

Marker, only when the user asks for one: `// qual:allow(srp, god_struct) reason:
"…"` above the struct.
